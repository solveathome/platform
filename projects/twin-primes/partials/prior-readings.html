01

Framing & research questions

A visual language for prime patterns

Chris’s independently developed perspective treats divisibility as overlapping periodic patterns. The repeating “tile” makes candidate prime pairs and the effect of each additional prime explicit.

The framework organizes classical sieve objects. Its exact recursions do not establish infinitely many twin primes.

02

Original observations & follow-through

Turning visual intuition into questions that can be checked

The observation log credits Chris with noticing the tile’s mirror-centre pattern and asking whether its symmetry could simplify the gap problem. It records the resulting divisibility explanation and the checks on the proposed shortcut.

The symmetry is exact; using half the pattern does not reduce the maximum-gap difficulty. These are documented observations and investigations, without a claim to mathematical priority.

03

Reusable research

Keeping the evidence, including what did not work

The programme preserves scripts, derivations, reviewed corrections, and an outcome register organized by research question. Each recorded approach gives the next researcher somewhere concrete to begin.

Finite computations retain their measured scope. A failed estimate is kept distinct from a refuted claim or a proof that every approach must fail.
